# Grandma's House

A first-person multiplayer survival game about living off the land on Grandma's homestead in Star Valley, Wyoming, through hard economic times. You grow food, preserve it, store it, trade it with transactional neighbors, and above all get through the winter. Every craft is done by hand. Grandma's notes, recipe cards and books teach you how; her story is never told directly.

## Survival is the game

Decided 2026-10-20 and merged into `main`; the old cozy game is archived as the git tag `cozy-final` (don't build on it). Modern times, hard economic times (not an apocalypse); you inherit Grandma's paid-off homestead and live off the land with up to three friends. Hard winters, food spoils, staples run out, and players can starve or freeze (collapse; a friend can bring you round; everyone down is game over). Neighbors are transactional and trade is barter (the store also keeps a ledger). Grandma's warmth is gone; her notes are a teaching aid. The goal each year is to survive the winter.

## Key decisions (summary; reasons are in the doc's Decision log)

- **Engine:** Unity 6 (6000.4.0f1), URP (Universal 3D template). Stylized, soft art.
- **Platforms:** PC first, with full controller support from day one. Consoles come after launch, and the Switch sets the performance budget.
- **Launch:** one mode, survival, 1 to 4 players (PC). The Cozy/Homesteader split is retired. Keep every number in data (ScriptableObjects) so difficulty settings can come later.
- **Stations:** the hands-on craft steps are stations (Schedule I style): cutting and packing jars. Brine is made by hand in the world: fill the pot at the sink, pour vinegar and shake salt from the pantry staples, set it on a burner. The camera moves into a working view, and you use tools directly. Moving things around happens in the world without entering a station: carrying jars, pots and the canner on and off the stove, the canner lid, and the canner itself (no station). The garden has no station either: plant, water (hold Interact with the can; it tips over where you look), pull weeds (hold Interact to grab, then drag the mouse or right stick back; the view holds still while the weed stretches until it pops out), and pick ripe fruit from where you stand. The one garden station is the close look at a single plant (`PlantStation`): press "Look closer" on a plant, turn it with Previous/Next or left/right, and pinch suckers, pick off hornworms, or pick what's ripe.
- **Carrying:** two hands, each holding a few things of one kind (`Pickup.perHand`: jalapeños 4, garlic/dill 3, tomatoes/onions 2, everything else 1); pots, the canner, baskets and the watering can take both hands (`Pickup.NeedsBothHands`). `PlayerHands.Held` is the last thing picked up, used first; `Carried` lists everything. Containers include the basket, egg basket, 12-jar crate, firewood carrier, watering can, and creel. A 4 to 6 slot tool belt is picked from a quick wheel. Stations reach into storage in the same room. No inventory screen; heavy things (logs) slow you instead of a weight limit.
- **Time:** a long real-time day in four parts; the clock exists only in the world (the kitchen clock, the wall calendar), never on screen. In survival the clock runs all night and a new day starts at midnight; sleeping fast-forwards until you're rested, and in a shared game the night passes faster only when everyone's in bed. The playtest year is three 5-day months and a 7-day winter with 20-minute days (22 days, about 7 hours; `CalendarSettings`, `TimeSettings`); a full-length year was 30-day months.
- **Meals:** hunger is real; raw produce feeds little, cooked meals feed well (variety and salt bonuses), spoiled food makes you sick.
- **Economy:** no cash. Neighbors barter (and keep the difference); the general store takes goods at 60% and keeps a credit ledger on Grandma's account (credit from goods only; it never lends). Staples run out: the store restocks only when a delivery comes in (`Trader.deliveryChanceByMonth`, 0.5/0.5/0.3/0.1 a day by month; each delivery brings 20-80% of each ware's max, rounded, so sometimes none).
- **Tone:** hard times, never named (no politics, war or apocalypse); no religion (only cultural staples). Grandma is fictional and appears only through text and a blurred painting.

## Code conventions

- C# scripts live in `Assets/_Project/Scripts/`, grouped by system (`Player/`, `Interaction/`, `Stations/`, `Carrying/`, `Garden/`, `Kitchen/`, `Audio/`, `UI/`, `Saving/`, `Time/`, `Data/`).
- Use the **new Input System** only, with one action map that works for both keyboard/mouse and gamepad.
- Never hard-code a key or button name in on-screen text; prompts go through the prompt-icon system (coming in Phase 1).
- Gameplay numbers (speeds, durations, capacities, mode values) live in ScriptableObjects under `Assets/_Project/Data/`, never hard-coded.
- Keep the scripts small and readable; match the style of the code around them.
- Prefer composition: stations, containers, and pickups are components on GameObjects.
- Dev menu (Editor and development builds only, `Scripts/DevTools/DevMenu` on the Homestead "Dev Tools" object): F1 while walking opens it; click a category (from each Pickup's `ItemCategory`) to see its items, pick how many, then click any item from `Data/SaveCatalog` to spawn it in front of the player. Items added to the catalog appear automatically; add future testing tools (time skip, etc.) to this menu rather than new hotkeys.
- Garden tending: a crop opts into suckers and pests through `CropData`'s Tending fields; both use the `Pinchable` component (taken off only in `PlantStation`'s close look) and appear at stage-prefab children named `SuckerPoint`/`PestPoint` (`HarvestPoint` for the harvest). A plant with suckers or crowding weeds loses its tended bonus that night (pruning only raises the yield). Crops can die (`ModeProfile.cropsCanDie`, on in survival; `GardenSettings` Neglect): a growing plant that goes to bed dry wilts (leans, fades) and dies after `dryNightsToDie` (2) dry nights running; one left with hornworms is chewed (yellows) and dies after `pestNightsToDie` (2). Seeds in dry soil just wait, and a ready single-harvest crop (potatoes, garlic...) doesn't die. A dead plant stands brown (`PlantSpot.IsDead`, saved); anything ripe on it can still be picked, and planting over it (`IsOpen`) pulls it out. There is no rain, so watering is a daily chore.
- Cutting board: produce set down while the board is busy waits in `WaitingRow` (up to six); a held basket empties onto it; the next piece rolls forward when one is finished. Pieces are named after their produce ("Onion Slice", "Tomato Wedge", "Clove"), which is how the pot and saves tell them apart.
- What a jar is: `JarContents.Recipe` picks the first recipe in the PintJar prefab's `recipes` list (Grandma's real recipes only) whose `needs`/`mostly` its contents meet; anything else is the `inedible` recipe, "Don't eat!" with a trash-can label. A new recipe needs its RecipeData, a place in that list, and a label (`Art/Textures/LabelText_*.png`: the picture with the name printed under it, drawn with System.Drawing).
- Handfuls: with empty hands, look at a pile on the cutting board and press Pick up for a `Handful` (a both-hands Pickup made at runtime; can't be set down, isn't saved). Drop it into an open jar or the cooking pot, or put it back on the board.
- Co-op groundwork (online co-op is being considered; see the doc's Co-op section): add no new statics or lone `FindAnyObjectByType` lookups for "the player" (pass the player in); route every world change through one method on its owner (like `GardenBed.PullOut(weed)`) so it can become a network call; keep visuals (stretch, wobble, steam) apart from state (pulled out, sealed); keep random rolls in one place the host can own.
- Survival needs: `Player/Needs` holds the rules as plain C# (hunger and rest 100 to 0, starving hours, sick hours) and `PlayerNeeds` runs them from `DayClock.TotalHours`; numbers are in `Data/SurvivalSettings`. Tired, malnourished or sick slows `FirstPersonController.SpeedMultiplier`; exhausted blocks brisk walking and makes you fumble one-handed items. Never forced to sleep; the bed fast-forwards time until you're rested, and "Get up" shows once the sun is up. Starving long enough (`collapseAfterHours`) makes you collapse, not die: another player can bring you round by holding food, looking at you and pressing Feed (`PlayerNet` is interactable; `PlayerNeeds.Fed`), and you come back still malnourished. When everyone has collapsed (or you, alone) the game is over (`ClockNet` tells each player; `NeedsView` shows it and leaves to the title). There's no health bar. Nothing shows on screen except edge darkening, a stomach growl, and the hold-Status check (`UI/NeedsView`). `Mode_Survival` turns these on and lets the clock run all night (`ModeProfile.clockStopsAtNight` off, new day at midnight).
- Root cellar and keeping crops: a cellar in the east yard (stairs down from the yard, a door, shelves and crates) with a `ColdStorage` at 0.2, so food keeps five times longer without power. Potatoes (from a sack of seed potatoes), carrots and winter squash grow like the other crops (single harvest, dug up when ready) and keep long: potatoes 30 days in the kitchen (150 in the cellar), carrots 14, squash 60. Eaten raw they feed little; they're meant for cooked meals. The ground is four slabs around the stairwell opening.
- Cooked meals: soup is the cooking pot with chopped vegetables in water (`CookingPot.IsSoup`; with vinegar it's still salsa). Hold an empty `Bowl` (four on the pantry's east shelf; `Bowl.plate` marks the four plates stacked beside them, which take skillet food and sauerkraut but never soup) and look at the pot to ladle a quarter-pot portion; eat it from your hands and the bowl stays. `Data/MealSettings` sets what each cooked piece is worth (potato and squash most), the variety and salt bonuses, and the uncooked share; `MealSettings.DishName` names it (Potato soup, Garden stew...). Raw produce feeds little on purpose (potato 4, onion 3, tomato 6), so cooking is how a harvest feeds people. Squash slices into rounds. Grandma's Potato Soup and Garden Stew cards are on the kitchen table.
- Chickens (`Scripts/Animals/`): four places for hens in a fenced run in the west yard (gate, henhouse with a pop door, three nest boxes, feeder, waterer). `Flock` (ISaveable, shared by `SharedState`; the host runs the nights) has each fed and watered hen lay an egg in a nest box overnight; a hen without food or water for `daysToStarve` dies; the fox takes a hen if the coop door is left open at night, or worse, if a hen was shut out (feathers show the next day). `Hen` wanders by day and goes in at dusk if the door's open (host moves her; others see her through a server NetworkTransform). `Feeder` takes a scoop from the feed sack, any produce (spoiled is fine), or the treat pail's hornworms; the waterer takes the watering can. Hornworms picked off the tomatoes go in the nearest `TreatPail`. Eggs (3 per hand, 21 days) go into the soup pot. Numbers are in `Data/FlockSettings`.
- Curing: `Kitchen/Curer` is the base for anything that cures food in its spots while working (the host runs it, over `cureHours`), via `Perishable.CureFor` → `CuredBy` (Dried, Smoked, Salted; keeps 30, 15, 40 times longer; dried shrinks, smoked browns, salted pales). Cured food goes straight into the soup pot as the pieces it would have been cut into (`CookingPot.AddDried`). The kinds: `Dehydrator` (side table under the kitchen's south window, 8 spots, switched on with empty hands, 12 h, needs power later); `Smokehouse` (east yard by the north fence, 4 hooks, light the fire with empty hands, it burns `fireHours` 10 then goes out, 16 h to smoke); `SaltBox` (root cellar beside the crock, 2 spots, meat only, pour in salt from the salt; works only with salt in it, each cured piece uses a scoop, 72 h).
- Meat: with empty hands, "Pick her up" off a hen in the run (`Flock.PickUp` hides the `Hen` as `Carried` and hands you a runtime `LiveHen` pickup, not saved); "Lay her on the block" at the `ChoppingBlock`, then one "Swing the axe" culls her (`LiveHen.Cull` → `Flock.Butcher(hen, at)`, guests ask the host through `FlockNet`). Set her down or drop her anywhere else and she lands right there (`Flock.LetGo(hen, at)`): in the run she's home, outside it she's `Loose`. A loose hen pecks about within `looseRange` of where she `Settled` (paths checked against fences and walls, never back into the run), roosts where she is at night (the fox's first pick), doesn't eat from the feeder or lay, and stays out until carried back. With the run gate open (`Flock.gate`, waypoints "Gate Inside"/"Gate Outside" in Yard/Coop) a hen in the run may wander out (`wanderOutChance`). The flock's saved/shared state holds `loose` and `settled` per hen (they change only on events, not as she walks). In co-op the flock's state carries each hen's `carried`/`carrier` (client id), so everyone hides her; `Flock.FreeLostHens` sends back a hen no one really holds (loaded mid-carry, or a friend who left). She's gone for good and leaves a `Chicken` (2 days fresh, slices on the board, 3 per piece in soup). `FreshFood.meat` makes raw meat sicken you unless it's cured.
- Fermenting (`Kitchen/Crock`, in the root cellar's northeast corner): cabbage is a crop (seeds in the garden shed) that slices into "Cabbage Slice" rounds. Pack handfuls from the board into the crock and shake in salt (one scoop per `shredsPerScoop` 12). The host ferments it each night (`DayClock.NewDay`); after `daysToFerment` (6) it's sauerkraut, worth more than salted cabbage. Too little salt for `daysToSpoil` (2) nights and it spoils. Adding fresh cabbage starts the count over. Scoop it into a `Bowl` to eat; a bowl scooped from spoiled kraut is rotten (`Bowl.Fill(..., spoiled)`). The crock is shared through `SharedState`.
- Calendar: `Data/CalendarSettings` lists the months in order with their length and band color (now Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter, 5 days each for the playtest year); `DateOf(day)` gives month, day of month and year, day 1 being the first month's first day, and the year repeats. `Time/WallCalendar` hangs on the kitchen's north wall left of the clock (text through `Art/Shaders/WorldText`, which is depth-tested; the built-in font shader draws through walls): it builds this month's page at runtime (TextMesh with the built-in font, quads for the grid), crosses off past days, rings today in red, and turns the page when `DayClock.Day` changes (checked each frame, so loads and guests catching up are covered). Use the calendar's month when seasons start to matter (winter).
- House size: the house was deepened from 4.2 m to 7.2 m (z -0.08 to 7.08; x -3.58 to 6.38 unchanged; the coop and cellar block widening). Everything along the north side (counters, stove, stations, pantry north shelves, dresser, beds) moved 3 m back; the table, fridge, freezer, stairs and doorways stayed. The roof keeps its 31 degree pitch, so the ridge is at about 7.1 m. The yard's north fence is at z 10. The kitchen has a second ceiling light.
- Journal: one journal for the whole household (`Interaction/Discoveries` on the root "Journal State" object, shared by a `SharedState`, saved). Reading a recipe card takes it into the journal: once anyone has read it, the card is gone for everyone (`Readable` hides found `RecipeCard` notes; loading an older save brings it back). Books stay where they are and give one How-to entry per page actually turned to (`NoteReader` calls `Discoveries.RecordPage`; a page's first short line is its title). The journal only records what you know; it never locks making anything. `UI/JournalView` (on the HUD, opened with the Journal action, J or d-pad up, or from the pause menu) has Recipes, How-to and Notes; Next/Previous go through entries, Interact turns sections, Back or Journal closes. Nothing shows for what hasn't been found (no hints). Sources are spread out: cards on the kitchen table and in the recipe box, Potato Soup and Sauerkraut on the root cellar shelves, Garden Stew in the pantry; booklets Keeping Hens (chicken run), Meat That Keeps (smokehouse), Drying Food (by the dehydrator), How to Can (kitchen bookshelf). A new note needs a NoteData under `Data/Notes`, an entry in `SaveCatalog.notes`, and a `Readable` in the world. Future: entries the player writes on their own (a log).
- Neighborhood and trade (`Scripts/Trade/`, scene root `World`): land all around the homestead's ground; a dirt lane from the gap in the yard's south fence (x 1-2.4) south to a crossroads at z -150 (about a minute's walk); Larsen's General Store just southeast (x 5-15, z -164 to -156; June behind the counter); Hansen Ranch 55 m east (Walt) and Sorensen Gardens 55 m west (Ada), each with a porch table and a bench. `Trader` (+ NetworkObject, SharedState, `TraderNet`): set goods on their counter (a trigger box; `OnCounter()` weighs what's there, baskets included) and talk to them with empty hands; `UI/TradeView` (on the HUD) shows both sides, +/- for their wares, and the balance. The store takes anything at `storeTakes` (60%) of its worth and keeps Grandma's ledger (`Credit`): extra goes on the account, the account covers a shortfall. Neighbors only take their `wants` (save keys, "Jar" for any sealed jar), keep the difference, ask `neighborAsks` (1.2x) for their things, and can trade a recipe card straight into the journal. Wares restock a little each day; traders are out by day only. The host makes every trade (guests send `TraderNet` RPCs). Worth lives in `Data/TradeSettings` (per item, with a per-month multiplier: eggs are dear in winter; sealed jars by recipe; cured food is worth more, turned food less, part-used staples by what's left). Staples run out: `Seasoning` holds `capacity` (salt 40 scoops, vinegar 2 pot-fulls), feed sacks and seed packets were already limited.
- Winter: `Time/WeatherModel` (plain C#) works every day's weather out of a seed: each calendar month's usual high/low (`Data/WeatherSettings`, Fahrenheit, blended toward the next month), a daily wander, and cold snaps in cold months (several days, much colder). Coldest before dawn, warmest mid-afternoon. `Time/Weather` (scene object, NetworkObject + SharedState sharing only the saved seed) gives the temperature now and `FeltAt(position, inBed)`: a `Shelter` you're inside (the house: outside + `houseHolds` + `stoveHeat` x the stove's heat; the root cellar a steady 45; the general store a steady 62), plus `besideStove` near a lit stove, plus `inBed`. On the host a killing frost (`killingFrost`) kills everything growing (`GardenBed.Frost`, ripe produce included) except under a frost cloth (`Garden/BedCover`, "Frost Cloth" rolled at each bed's near end: Interact pulls it over or rolls it back; `GardenBed.Covered`, saved and sent like other bed changes). The cloth holds down to `coverHolds` (5) degrees below a killing frost; colder is a hard freeze and kills everything (the radio says "Hard freeze" instead of "Killing frost"). Nothing can be planted, watered or picked under the cloth, so a bed left covered dries out. Frozen ground (`GroundFrozen`: today's high under `groundFrozenBelow`) takes no seed. Warmth is a need in `Player/Needs` (100 to 0): it drains below `comfortableAbove` (55) by `coldPerDegreeHour` per degree, recovers when warm, and `freezeCollapseHours` (2) at none collapses you; a friend with empty hands can "Warm them up" (`PlayerNet.WarmRpc`, `PlayerNeeds.WarmedUp`); food doesn't get you up from the cold. Cues: frost creeping in at the screen's edges, a Warmth line in the hold-Status check, cold slows you. `Kitchen/WoodStove` (kitchen west wall): put firewood in (holds 4), light it with empty hands; each log burns `hoursPerLog` (4) on the host; its `Heat` ramps up over an hour and fades over three after it goes out. Firewood (`Kitchen/Firewood`, 3 per hand) only comes from splitting logs (below). The radio's second button gives the farm report (`Weather.Forecast()`, shown by `UI/Caption`): now, today and the next three days, warning of cold snaps and killing frost. Scene things that run on clock hours must reset their last-seen time on a clock jump (`IAfterLoad` and `DayClock.Jumped`), or a load spends days at once.
- Wood and the truck: Walt sells logs (`Kitchen/Log`: both hands, `carrySpeed` 0.35, no brisk walking; 8 in stock, 1.5 a day), set out on his log pile by the road (`Trader.Ware.setOutAt`). Grandma's old pickup (`Vehicles/Truck`, scene `World/Truck`, parked at the end of the lane) is driven with the Move action: get in with empty hands ("Drive the truck"), look around freely, Interact to get out ("Get out", via `ISeat` on `PlayerHands.Seat`; `FirstPersonController.Seated` stops your body moving). Arcade handling (`topSpeed` 11, steering only while rolling); `Blocked` box-casts its body (above wheel height, ignoring itself and loose items) so it stops at walls. Shared: `TruckNet` hands the truck's NetworkObject to the driver (owner NetworkTransform), one driver at a time. `TruckBed` (6 spots) loads logs or firewood that ride along; take them out by picking them up. The chopping block (`Kitchen/ChoppingBlock`, by the end of the lane, with Grandma's `Axe`): stand a log on it, then swing the axe; three swings split it into 4 firewood (each swing costs rest); the host makes the firewood (`ChoppingBlockNet`).
- The year's story: `UI/StoryCard` (scene "Story Card", its own overlay canvas) shows a new game's opening text (why you left the city; `SaveSlot.NewGame`, set by the title screen's New game, or a guest joining on day 1) and, the first morning of spring after a winter, "You made it through the winter" with the tally. `Time/YearTally` (scene "Year Tally", SharedState, saved) counts jars sealed, eggs laid, firewood split and times someone went down (`YearTally.Add`, called from those places); the host closes the year on spring's first day (`LastYear`, `Closed`). Input is off while a card shows; Interact or Back skips it.
- Night and snow: `SunCycle.Darkness` (0 day, 1 night) drives `Time/NightLight` lamps (porch lights at the house, Walt's and Ada's, the store lamp) and the truck's `headlights` (on with someone in it after dark). `WeatherModel` gives each day `snow` (a cold gray day, `snowBelow`, `snowChance` per month, never a cold snap) and `SnowCover(day)` (piles up per snowy day, melts on days above `meltAbove`). `Time/SnowCover` (on Weather) tints the grass, roofs, lane and truck toward white with property blocks; `Time/Snowfall` (scene "Snowfall", Proto_Snow particles) follows the camera and stops inside a `Shelter`; snowy days are overcast (`SunCycle.overcastSun/overcastSky`); the radio mentions snow.
- Chicks: a new game's coop is empty (`Flock.startingHens` 0; `Flock.Awake` marks the rest not alive). Hens come as chicks from Ada: a `ChickBox` item (key "ChickBox", worth 6; cardboard box with a yellow head peeking out). Set it down and use its second action, "Let the chick out" (only with room in the coop): `Flock.Adopt` (guests via `FlockNet`) fills the first empty hen place with a chick there, in the run or loose. `Hen.AgeDays` counts fed nights (saved as `ages`; older saves load grown); under `FlockSettings.daysToLay` (5) she's a chick: half size, yellow, no comb, eats `chickShare` (half) rations, doesn't lay, and can't go on the chopping block. Ada gives one chick free the first time you talk to her (`Trader.welcomeGift`/`welcomeLine`, `Welcomed` saved; guests via `TraderNet`). Test helper `Homestead.Load(hens: true)` (the default) fills the coop with grown hens; chick tests load with `hens: false`.
- Neighbors each have one trade: Walt sells only logs (and his recipe card), Ada only chicks (and hers), and every seed packet (and seed potatoes and chicken feed) comes from the store, on its delivery truck.
- Outdoor sound: `Audio/Outdoors` (root "Outdoor Sounds") follows the camera and plays flat loops of wind (calm, winter, snow levels) and crickets (summer and fall nights) plus birdsong one-shots by day outside winter, all muffled inside a `Shelter`. The truck runs `SoundId.Engine` while occupied (pitch with speed); hens cluck. New `SoundId`s go at the end of the enum. Hens lay by `FlockSettings.layChance` per month (`Flock.calendar`).
- Food (`Scripts/Food/`): `FreshFood` (produce, eaten raw) and `JarFood` (eat the whole jar, which leaves it empty) implement `IEdible`; holding one, the Pick up button says "Eat". `Perishable` ages food by clock hours (`shelfLifeDays` at room temperature) and darkens it past half-fresh; rotten food or an unsafe jar makes you sick instead of feeding you. A sealed jar doesn't spoil. `ColdStorage` is a trigger box (fridge rate about 0.3, freezer 0). Keep `Perishable` the last `IItemState` on a prefab so older saves line up.
- Multiplayer (Netcode for GameObjects 2.x + Steamworks.NET): up to 4 players; whoever starts the game hosts it (solo play is hosting alone), and friends join through a friends-only Steam lobby (invite from the pause menu, or Join Game in Steam). `Scripts/Net/`: `NetSession` (on `Resources/NetSession.prefab` with the NetworkManager, UnityTransport and our `SteamTransport`), `HomesteadSession` (opening the Homestead hosts), `PlayerNet` (owner gets camera/input/HUD via `IPlayerBound`; others see a figure), `ItemNet` (on every item prefab: the host decides who holds what; the holder owns and moves it; state is shared via `IItemState` when it's let go), `ClockNet` (host keeps time; the night passes only when every player is in bed; the host saves when it ends), `Bed` (one sleeper each). Guests can only use things marked `ISharedUse` (items lying loose or in a basket, beds, the fridge/freezer doors, the garden, and the kitchen: stations, stove, pot, canner, sink, towels, shelves and labels). Items lying on a station are taken through the station. Kitchen sharing: whoever last handled an item runs it (`ItemNet.Simulates`; gate anything that changes over time with `ItemNet.Runs(this)`) and sends its states plus where it sits (parent path, container) every 0.3 s when they change; a non-owner may only send content changes (`ProposeRpc`), never where it sits. Applying a place reparents the item and calls `IAfterLoad.AfterLoad()` on the station there, just like loading. Scene ISaveables (cutting board, burners) use `SharedState` the same way, and a `SharedState` on a station also locks it to one player at a time. Remove items for good with `ItemNet.Remove`, never `Destroy`. The garden (`GardenNet` on each bed): the host grows it (nights, weeds, suckers, pests) and sends `GardenBed.Capture()` whenever it changes; guests apply only what changed (`ApplyShared`), and a guest's planting, watering, weeding and pinching show at once and go to the host through `GardenBed.Plant/Water/PullOut/TakeOff`. Ripe produce is spawned as shared items. A new item prefab needs NetworkObject (AutoObjectParentSync off, DontDestroyWithOwner on) + owner-authority NetworkTransform + ItemNet, and an entry in `Data/Network/NetworkPrefabs`. Copying a scene object with a NetworkObject duplicates its GlobalObjectIdHash: re-run NetworkObject.OnValidate and save. Sleepers lie in their bed (`Bed` turns off the CharacterController and places you; you get out on a free side) and collapsed players lie on the floor; other players see the figure lying down (`PlayerNet.Pose`). Test two players on one PC with a development build: host in the editor without Steam (create `Temp/GH_DIRECT`, delete it after), run the build with `-direct -join 127.0.0.1 -bot` (DevBot logs `[bot]` steps). Steam can't start twice in one process, so the editor keeps Steam running between Play sessions. The Steam app ID is 480 (Spacewar) in `steam_appid.txt` until the game has its own; copy that file next to built .exe files.
- Testing without the editor bridge: the editor's PlayMode runs have frozen Unity (main thread idle, around the garden tests) when started through MCP for Unity; the same tests pass in batch mode. With the editor closed: `Unity.exe -batchmode -projectPath <project> -runTests -testPlatform PlayMode|EditMode -testResults <xml> -logFile <log>` (add `-testFilter "GrandmasHouse.Tests.X"`). `InputTests.HoldingWWalksForward` always fails in batch mode (no keyboard), fine in the editor. `Tests/PlayMode/Pictures` (explicit, run by name with the `GH_SHOTS` folder set) saves screenshots of the cards, night and snow. Scene wiring can also be done in batch mode with a temporary editor script and `-executeMethod` (delete the script after).
- Saving: a new item prefab needs a `SavedItem` key and an entry in `Data/SaveCatalog`; extra item state implements `IItemState`, scene systems implement `ISaveable`, and stations that hold things re-adopt them in `IAfterLoad`.

## Folder layout

```
Assets/_Project/
  Scripts/      gameplay code, grouped by system; compiles as the GrandmasHouse assembly (GrandmasHouse.asmdef)
  Tests/        EditMode/ (rules, no scene) and PlayMode/ (full loops) test assemblies; Code Coverage package installed
  Data/         ScriptableObject assets (items, recipes, mode profiles)
  Prefabs/
  Scenes/       Title.unity starts the game; Homestead.unity is the main scene (kitchen, pantry, bedroom, garden); Prototype.unity is the old test room
```

- Never mark anything that moves Static (door and gate leaves under a `HingedDoor`, the truck, hens): Unity bakes static meshes in place, so the hinge turns but the door stays drawn shut. `DoorTests` checks the doors.
- Jars can fail to seal (`ModeProfile.jarsCanFailToSeal`, on in survival): as a jar cools out of the canner, its lid fails `JarContents.sealFailChance` (4%) of the time, or `badHeadspaceSealFail` (60%) when it wasn't filled to the line (`JarQuality.HeadspaceOff`). No ping, it isn't sealed (spoils like fresh food, won't go on the pantry shelf), and it can go through the canner again.
- Firewood: split pieces are wedges (`Art/Meshes/FirewoodSplit`, bark on the curved back, `Proto_SplitWood` faces). `Kitchen/Woodpile` ("Yard/Woodpile", a rack on the south wall of the house under the kitchen window, 20 spots in 4 rows) holds real firewood pieces in named spots ("Spot 1".. so saves find them); stack with Interact on the rack or the second action anywhere on the pile, take pieces like any item. The smokehouse burns firewood like the stove (`capacity` 4, `hoursPerLog` 4; cureHours 16 = a full pit); its curing counts for the hours it actually burned even when a long jump (sleep) burns it out.
- West yard: the coop, run, hens and feed sack were moved 2.12 m west (run floor x -8.72 to -6.52) and the yard's west fence moved out to x -9.12 (one more rail section on the north and south sides), leaving about 2.9 m between the house and the coop. A save from before the move puts in-run hens on the new run's floor (`Flock.Restore`).
- Jar quality: `Data/JarQuality` grades a jar against its recipe like a county fair: 0 marks is a blue ribbon, 1 red, 2 white, 3+ none. Marks: not packed full (`packedEnough`), brine/salsa not at the jar's line (`lineTolerance`), brine vinegar share off (`vinegarTolerance`), salt per full pot off (`saltTolerance`), short on garlic, no peppercorns. What a jar *is* (dill, lemon) stays `JarRecipe`'s job. The pot tracks salt as an amount that pours out with the brine (`CookingPot.SaltPerFullPot`, `VinegarShare`); jars average what was poured into them (`BrineVinegar`, `BrineSalt`, saved). A labeled jar wears a rosette on its lid (`Kitchen/Marks`), and `TradeSettings.ribbonWorth` scales what neighbors pay. Jars have a fill line on the glass; the pot already had its Vinegar and Full marks. Recipe cards are written in game amounts with `{garlic}`, `{slices}`, `{wedges}`, `{cloves}`, `{salt}`, `{minutes}` filled from the recipe.
- Kitchen timer: `Kitchen/KitchenTimer` ("House/Kitchen/Kitchen Timer" on the counter by the stove) reads the canner: wound back while a load processes (`Canner.Processing`, `ProcessLeft`/`ProcessTotal`), rings (`SoundId.TimerBell`, carries 45 m) when it's done, including after sleeping.
- Tasting: eating a sealed jar of a real recipe (`JarFood.Taste`) shows a caption like "Dill Pickle Chips: a red ribbon jar. Too salty; go easier on the salt." and keeps the latest note per recipe in `Discoveries` (saved, shared), shown in the journal's Tasting tab. "Needs X" is fine after the fact, never as a hint while making something.
- Moving goods: `JarCrate` (12 pints, pantry floor), `BurlapSack` (produce, 30, by the back door) and `EggBasket` (12 eggs, by the coop) are `Container` prefabs copied from the harvest basket (saved, networked, sellable as a lot). The truck bed hauls anything with a `Container` as well as wood; contents ride inside.
- Marks built in code (`Kitchen/Marks`) must remove their colliders with `DestroyImmediate`: a `Pickup` caches its colliders in Awake, and a collider pending destruction there left jars hanging in the air when set down.
- Canning kit: `Kitchen/CanningKit` ("House/Kitchen/Canning Kit", saved as "canning-kit", shared) hides and un-solids its parts (Jar Packing towel and lids, Hot Pad and spot, Cooling Towel and spots, the table's Label Box) when put away, leaving whatever sat on them where it was. Put away: second action on the packing towel (`CanningPutAway`) with empty hands, which hands you a `CanningBox` item. Set up: hold the box and look at the counter (`CanningCounter`). A new game starts put away (`startsSetUp` off), the box ("Items/Canning Box") on the pantry floor beside the jar crates. `Homestead.Load()` in tests sets the kit up unless asked not to (`canningSetUp: false`). Don't deactivate the parts (they carry NetworkObjects); hide renderers and colliders instead.
- Lids: a lid comes back off until the jar is canned (`JarContents.TakeOffLid`, second action on the jar, or point at the lid on the towel). On the towel you take a lid from the stack and put it on the jar you choose; while pouring, the pot follows the cursor to the jar you aim at.
- A loose `Pickup` lying still, unparented, held by no one and not gliding always turns its colliders back on (`Pickup.Update`, `HeldBy` set by PlayerHands).
- First-year balance (design doc tab "First-year balance plan"): solo is the target for now. Hunger 3.6 an hour awake (~60 a day, three meals). You start with 42 seeds (potatoes 6, carrots 6, tomato, cucumber, dill, garlic, onion 4 each, squash and cabbage 3, jalapeño and marigold 2); bought packets hold 10. All seed packets and seed potatoes come from the store (on its delivery truck). The store sells `CannedBeans` (25 food, keeps a year, worth 15, 2 in stock) as a dear fallback paid from store credit. The radio calls a killing frost only the day before.
- Skillet: `Kitchen/Skillet` (prefab Prefabs/Kitchen/Skillet, saved as "Skillet", starts on Burner 3 of the stove; burners claim a pan in their spot at Start). Eggs cracked in (an egg takes 2 of 24 places), handfuls dropped in, or carry it to the cutting board to scrape in; a pinch of salt. On a lit burner it fries after 12 s and burns after 40 s (food x0.4). Food = `MealSettings.PotFood` x0.85 once fried; over 30 food serves 2 plates, else 1, onto an empty plate ("Serve a plate"). Doneness shows by look (`Skillet.Stage`: Raw, Cooking "Half-cooked", Done, Burning, Burnt; `Cooked` 0-1 then `Charred` 0-1): egg whites clear-gray to white with a golden `Edge`, yolk orange to yellow; other pieces go golden; past done they brown then blacken, and the `smoke` particles go from steam to dark smoke. Dish names: Fried eggs and potatoes, Potato and onion hash, Fried chicken, Fried squash (and onions), Fried egg(s), Fried potatoes. Four recipe cards (Note_FriedEggsCard, Note_HashCard, Note_FriedChickenCard, Note_FriedSquashCard) lie by the kitchen recipe box.
- Cutting: `Sliceable.cut` is Rounds (cucumber, carrot, jalapeño...), Cubes (potato, winter squash) or Diced (onion). Cubes and dice are named "<Produce> Cube" (kind is still the first word), one piece per cut like a slice so food values don't change, and heap up in a small mound on the board (`CuttingBoardStation.CubeSpot`). On the packing towel any head of dill can be picked up, the one nearest the cursor.
- Starting food: the house holds no fresh food (it would all have spoiled while Grandma's place stood empty). What's left is shelf-stable, on the pantry's eye-level shelf (Shelves East, Slots 1-7): five of Grandma's own sealed, labeled jars (`Kitchen/GrandmasJar` sets each one's `JarContents` state on a new game: two canned tomatoes, two dill pickle chips, a salsa) and two `CannedBeans`, about 190 food, three days at ~60 a day. Eggs come from the hens; the rest is the garden.
- Garden shed ("Yard/Garden Shed", a board shed east of the garden beds, doorway facing west toward them): the seed packets sit in a row on the shelf on its back wall and the watering can stands just inside the door. A `HingedDoor` ("Door Hinge", save key "shed-door", NetworkObject) swings out toward the garden. Two burlap sacks and the harvest basket sit inside too.
