# Grandma's House

A first-person comfort game about living Grandma's homestead life in Star Valley, Wyoming. You grow food, preserve it, store it, and share it through the seasons. Every task is done by hand at a "station". Grandma's story is discovered through the notes, diary, recipe cards, and jars she left behind, never told directly.

## This is the `survival` branch

This folder (`C:\Dev\GrandmasHouse-Survival`, a git worktree of the same repo) explores a new direction: a harder, possibly multiplayer survival game. Modern times, hard economic times (not an apocalypse), and you inherit Grandma's paid-off homestead and live off the land. Hard winters, crop blight and animal disease, food spoils, and players can starve and die. Neighbors are transactional and everything is barter. Grandma's warmth is gone; her notes become a teaching aid. The goal each year is to survive the winter. Nothing is decided yet: the ideas and open questions are in the design doc's **"Direction brainstorm (open)"** tab, and the decision log should record what gets settled. The cozy game stays on `main` in `C:\Dev\GrandmasHouse`; don't change it from here.

Where the cozy rules below conflict with survival (no pressure or danger, meals as bonuses only, no hard times, Cozy mode only), treat them as **under review on this branch**, not as rules.

## The design doc is the source of truth

**Design doc:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/e30813e2-ed8f-439c-8954-9add1553c5e3 (a Claude Doc; read and edit it with the Claude Docs tools, never by web fetch).

Every session working on this project must:

1. **Read before working.** Check the design doc sections that relate to the task, plus "Roadmap → Current phase".
2. **Update after working.**
   - Tick off finished items under Current phase.
   - Bring any section that changed up to date.
   - Add a row to the **Decision log** (Date · Decision · Why) for every meaningful decision, newest first. When a decision changes, add a new row; don't rewrite old rows.
3. **No hand-holding.** Don't block steps until earlier ones are done, and don't show reminders like "needs salt" or "add lemon juice first". Let the player do things in any order or skip them; the result shows in what they made (a jar that isn't one of Grandma's recipes is "Don't eat!"). Button prompts for what you can do right now are fine.
4. **Test every idea** against: *Does this make you feel like you're living off the land the way Grandma did, and that it's hard?* Pressure and danger are allowed here; make them telegraphed and survivable through planning, not reflexes.

## Key decisions (summary; reasons are in the doc's Decision log)

- **Engine:** Unity 6 (6000.4.0f1), URP (Universal 3D template). Stylized, soft art.
- **Platforms:** PC first, with full controller support from day one. Consoles come after launch, and the Switch sets the performance budget.
- **Launch:** Cozy mode only. Homesteader (hard mode) is parked. Keep numbers in data (ScriptableObjects) so it stays possible, but build nothing that only Homesteader needs.
- **Stations:** the hands-on craft steps are stations (Schedule I style): cutting and packing jars. Brine is made by hand in the world: fill the pot at the sink, pour vinegar and shake salt from the pantry staples, set it on a burner. The camera moves into a working view, and you use tools directly. Moving things around happens in the world without entering a station: carrying jars, pots and the canner on and off the stove, the canner lid, and the canner itself (no station). The garden has no station either: plant, water (hold Interact with the can; it tips over where you look), pull weeds (hold Interact to grab, then drag the mouse or right stick back; the view holds still while the weed stretches until it pops out), and pick ripe fruit from where you stand. The one garden station is the close look at a single plant (`PlantStation`): press "Look closer" on a plant, turn it with Previous/Next or left/right, and pinch suckers, pick off hornworms, or pick what's ripe.
- **Carrying:** two hands, each holding a few things of one kind (`Pickup.perHand`: jalapeños 4, garlic/dill 3, tomatoes/onions 2, everything else 1); pots, the canner, baskets and the watering can take both hands (`Pickup.NeedsBothHands`). `PlayerHands.Held` is the last thing picked up, used first; `Carried` lists everything. Containers include the basket, egg basket, 12-jar crate, firewood carrier, watering can, and creel. A 4 to 6 slot tool belt is picked from a quick wheel. Stations reach into storage in the same room. No weight limits, no inventory screen.
- **Time:** a long real-time day (about 40 to 60 minutes, to be tuned) in four parts. The clock exists only in the world, never on screen. The sun stops once night falls, and the next day starts when you sleep. Meals don't move time forward.
- **Meals:** bonuses only, never penalties (like Valheim's food), plus a variety bonus.
- **Economy:** no cash. Neighbors barter directly; the general store keeps a credit ledger on Grandma's account.
- **Tone:** no religion (only cultural staples), no politics, war, or hard times. Grandma is fictional and appears only through text and a blurred painting.

## Code conventions

- C# scripts live in `Assets/_Project/Scripts/`, grouped by system (`Player/`, `Interaction/`, `Stations/`, `Carrying/`, `Garden/`, `Kitchen/`, `Audio/`, `UI/`, `Saving/`, `Time/`, `Data/`).
- Use the **new Input System** only, with one action map that works for both keyboard/mouse and gamepad.
- Never hard-code a key or button name in on-screen text; prompts go through the prompt-icon system (coming in Phase 1).
- Gameplay numbers (speeds, durations, capacities, mode values) live in ScriptableObjects under `Assets/_Project/Data/`, never hard-coded.
- Keep the scripts small and readable; match the style of the code around them.
- Prefer composition: stations, containers, and pickups are components on GameObjects.
- Dev menu (Editor and development builds only, `Scripts/DevTools/DevMenu` on the Homestead "Dev Tools" object): F1 while walking opens it; click a category (from each Pickup's `ItemCategory`) to see its items, pick how many, then click any item from `Data/SaveCatalog` to spawn it in front of the player. Items added to the catalog appear automatically; add future testing tools (time skip, etc.) to this menu rather than new hotkeys.
- Garden tending: a crop opts into suckers and pests through `CropData`'s Tending fields; both use the `Pinchable` component (taken off only in `PlantStation`'s close look) and appear at stage-prefab children named `SuckerPoint`/`PestPoint` (`HarvestPoint` for the harvest). A plant with suckers, pests or crowding weeds loses its tended bonus that night.
- Cutting board: produce set down while the board is busy waits in `WaitingRow` (up to six); a held basket empties onto it; the next piece rolls forward when one is finished. Pieces are named after their produce ("Onion Slice", "Tomato Wedge", "Clove"), which is how the pot and saves tell them apart.
- What a jar is: `JarContents.Recipe` picks the first recipe in the PintJar prefab's `recipes` list (Grandma's real recipes only) whose `needs`/`mostly` its contents meet; anything else is the `inedible` recipe, "Don't eat!" with a trash-can label. A new recipe needs its RecipeData, a place in that list, and a label (`Art/Textures/LabelText_*.png`: the picture with the name printed under it, drawn with System.Drawing).
- Handfuls: with empty hands, look at a pile on the cutting board and press Pick up for a `Handful` (a both-hands Pickup made at runtime; can't be set down, isn't saved). Drop it into an open jar or the cooking pot, or put it back on the board.
- Co-op groundwork (online co-op is being considered; see the doc's Co-op section): add no new statics or lone `FindAnyObjectByType` lookups for "the player" (pass the player in); route every world change through one method on its owner (like `GardenBed.PullOut(weed)`) so it can become a network call; keep visuals (stretch, wobble, steam) apart from state (pulled out, sealed); keep random rolls in one place the host can own.
- Survival needs (survival branch): `Player/Needs` holds the rules as plain C# (hunger and rest 100 to 0, starving hours, sick hours) and `PlayerNeeds` runs them from `DayClock.TotalHours`; numbers are in `Data/SurvivalSettings`. Tired, malnourished or sick slows `FirstPersonController.SpeedMultiplier`; exhausted blocks brisk walking and makes you fumble one-handed items. Never forced to sleep; the bed fast-forwards time until you're rested, and "Get up" shows once the sun is up. Starving long enough (`collapseAfterHours`) makes you collapse, not die: another player can bring you round by holding food, looking at you and pressing Feed (`PlayerNet` is interactable; `PlayerNeeds.Fed`), and you come back still malnourished. When everyone has collapsed (or you, alone) the game is over (`ClockNet` tells each player; `NeedsView` shows it and leaves to the title). There's no health bar. Nothing shows on screen except edge darkening, a stomach growl, and the hold-Status check (`UI/NeedsView`). `Mode_Survival` turns these on and lets the clock run all night (`ModeProfile.clockStopsAtNight` off, new day at midnight).
- Root cellar and keeping crops (survival branch): a cellar in the east yard (stairs down from the yard, a door, shelves and crates) with a `ColdStorage` at 0.2, so food keeps five times longer without power. Potatoes (from a sack of seed potatoes), carrots and winter squash grow like the other crops (single harvest, dug up when ready) and keep long: potatoes 30 days in the kitchen (150 in the cellar), carrots 14, squash 60. Eaten raw they feed little; they're meant for cooked meals. The ground is four slabs around the stairwell opening.
- Cooked meals (survival branch): soup is the cooking pot with chopped vegetables in water (`CookingPot.IsSoup`; with vinegar it's still salsa). Hold an empty `Bowl` (four on the pantry's east shelf) and look at the pot to ladle a quarter-pot portion; eat it from your hands and the bowl stays. `Data/MealSettings` sets what each cooked piece is worth (potato and squash most), the variety and salt bonuses, and the uncooked share; `MealSettings.DishName` names it (Potato soup, Garden stew...). Raw produce feeds little on purpose (potato 4, onion 3, tomato 6), so cooking is how a harvest feeds people. Squash slices into rounds. Grandma's Potato Soup and Garden Stew cards are on the kitchen table.
- Chickens (survival branch, `Scripts/Animals/`): four hens in a fenced run in the west yard (gate, henhouse with a pop door, three nest boxes, feeder, waterer). `Flock` (ISaveable, shared by `SharedState`; the host runs the nights) has each fed and watered hen lay an egg in a nest box overnight; a hen without food or water for `daysToStarve` dies; the fox takes a hen if the coop door is left open at night, or worse, if a hen was shut out (feathers show the next day). `Hen` wanders by day and goes in at dusk if the door's open (host moves her; others see her through a server NetworkTransform). `Feeder` takes a scoop from the feed sack, any produce (spoiled is fine), or the treat pail's hornworms; the waterer takes the watering can. Hornworms picked off the tomatoes go in the nearest `TreatPail`. Eggs (3 per hand, 21 days) go into the soup pot. Numbers are in `Data/FlockSettings`.
- Curing (survival branch): `Kitchen/Curer` is the base for anything that cures food in its spots while working (the host runs it, over `cureHours`), via `Perishable.CureFor` → `CuredBy` (Dried, Smoked, Salted; keeps 30, 15, 40 times longer; dried shrinks, smoked browns, salted pales). Cured food goes straight into the soup pot as the pieces it would have been cut into (`CookingPot.AddDried`). The kinds: `Dehydrator` (side table under the kitchen's south window, 8 spots, switched on with empty hands, 12 h, needs power later); `Smokehouse` (east yard by the north fence, 4 hooks, light the fire with empty hands, it burns `fireHours` 10 then goes out, 16 h to smoke); `SaltBox` (root cellar beside the crock, 2 spots, meat only, pour in salt from the salt; works only with salt in it, each cured piece uses a scoop, 72 h).
- Meat (survival branch): with empty hands, look at a hen out in the run and "Take her for the pot" (`Hen` is interactable; `Flock.Butcher`, guests ask the host through `FlockNet`). She's gone for good and leaves a `Chicken` (2 days fresh, slices on the board, 3 per piece in soup). `FreshFood.meat` makes raw meat sicken you unless it's cured.
- Fermenting (survival branch, `Kitchen/Crock`, in the root cellar's northeast corner): cabbage is a crop (seeds on the kitchen table, two heads on the cellar shelf) that slices into "Cabbage Slice" rounds. Pack handfuls from the board into the crock and shake in salt (one scoop per `shredsPerScoop` 12). The host ferments it each night (`DayClock.NewDay`); after `daysToFerment` (6) it's sauerkraut, worth more than salted cabbage. Too little salt for `daysToSpoil` (2) nights and it spoils. Adding fresh cabbage starts the count over. Scoop it into a `Bowl` to eat; a bowl scooped from spoiled kraut is rotten (`Bowl.Fill(..., spoiled)`). The crock is shared through `SharedState`.
- Calendar (survival branch): `Data/CalendarSettings` lists the months in order with their length and band color (now Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter, 30 days each); `DateOf(day)` gives month, day of month and year, day 1 being the first month's first day, and the year repeats. `Time/WallCalendar` hangs on the kitchen's north wall left of the clock (text through `Art/Shaders/WorldText`, which is depth-tested; the built-in font shader draws through walls): it builds this month's page at runtime (TextMesh with the built-in font, quads for the grid), crosses off past days, rings today in red, and turns the page when `DayClock.Day` changes (checked each frame, so loads and guests catching up are covered). Use the calendar's month when seasons start to matter (winter).
- House size (survival branch): the house was deepened from 4.2 m to 7.2 m (z -0.08 to 7.08; x -3.58 to 6.38 unchanged; the coop and cellar block widening). Everything along the north side (counters, stove, stations, pantry north shelves, dresser, beds) moved 3 m back; the table, fridge, freezer, stairs and doorways stayed. The roof keeps its 31 degree pitch, so the ridge is at about 7.1 m. The yard's north fence is at z 10. The kitchen has a second ceiling light.
- Journal (survival branch): one journal for the whole household (`Interaction/Discoveries` on the root "Journal State" object, shared by a `SharedState`, saved). Reading a recipe card takes it into the journal: once anyone has read it, the card is gone for everyone (`Readable` hides found `RecipeCard` notes; loading an older save brings it back). Books stay where they are and give one How-to entry per page actually turned to (`NoteReader` calls `Discoveries.RecordPage`; a page's first short line is its title). The journal only records what you know; it never locks making anything. `UI/JournalView` (on the HUD, opened with the Journal action, J or d-pad up, or from the pause menu) has Recipes, How-to and Notes; Next/Previous go through entries, Interact turns sections, Back or Journal closes. Nothing shows for what hasn't been found (no hints). Sources are spread out: cards on the kitchen table and in the recipe box, Potato Soup and Sauerkraut on the root cellar shelves, Garden Stew in the pantry; booklets Keeping Hens (chicken run), Meat That Keeps (smokehouse), Drying Food (by the dehydrator), How to Can (kitchen bookshelf). A new note needs a NoteData under `Data/Notes`, an entry in `SaveCatalog.notes`, and a `Readable` in the world. Future: entries the player writes on their own (a log).
- Neighborhood and trade (survival branch, `Scripts/Trade/`, scene root `World`): land all around the homestead's ground; a dirt lane from the gap in the yard's south fence (x 1-2.4) south to a crossroads at z -150 (about a minute's walk); Larsen's General Store just southeast (x 5-15, z -164 to -156; June behind the counter); Hansen Ranch 55 m east (Walt) and Sorensen Gardens 55 m west (Ada), each with a porch table and a bench. `Trader` (+ NetworkObject, SharedState, `TraderNet`): set goods on their counter (a trigger box; `OnCounter()` weighs what's there, baskets included) and talk to them with empty hands; `UI/TradeView` (on the HUD) shows both sides, +/- for their wares, and the balance. The store takes anything at `storeTakes` (60%) of its worth and keeps Grandma's ledger (`Credit`): extra goes on the account, the account covers a shortfall. Neighbors only take their `wants` (save keys, "Jar" for any sealed jar), keep the difference, ask `neighborAsks` (1.2x) for their things, and can trade a recipe card straight into the journal. Wares restock a little each day; traders are out by day only. The host makes every trade (guests send `TraderNet` RPCs). Worth lives in `Data/TradeSettings` (per item, with a per-month multiplier: eggs are dear in winter; sealed jars by recipe; cured food is worth more, turned food less, part-used staples by what's left). Staples run out: `Seasoning` holds `capacity` (salt 40 scoops, vinegar 2 pot-fulls), feed sacks and seed packets were already limited.
- Winter (survival branch): `Time/WeatherModel` (plain C#) works every day's weather out of a seed: each calendar month's usual high/low (`Data/WeatherSettings`, Fahrenheit, blended toward the next month), a daily wander, and cold snaps in cold months (several days, much colder). Coldest before dawn, warmest mid-afternoon. `Time/Weather` (scene object, NetworkObject + SharedState sharing only the saved seed) gives the temperature now and `FeltAt(position, inBed)`: a `Shelter` you're inside (the house: outside + `houseHolds` + `stoveHeat` x the stove's heat; the root cellar a steady 45; the general store a steady 62), plus `besideStove` near a lit stove, plus `inBed`. On the host a killing frost (`killingFrost`) kills everything growing (`GardenBed.Frost`, ripe produce included), and frozen ground (`GroundFrozen`: today's high under `groundFrozenBelow`) takes no seed. Warmth is a need in `Player/Needs` (100 to 0): it drains below `comfortableAbove` (55) by `coldPerDegreeHour` per degree, recovers when warm, and `freezeCollapseHours` (2) at none collapses you; a friend with empty hands can "Warm them up" (`PlayerNet.WarmRpc`, `PlayerNeeds.WarmedUp`); food doesn't get you up from the cold. Cues: frost creeping in at the screen's edges, a Warmth line in the hold-Status check, cold slows you. `Kitchen/WoodStove` (kitchen west wall): put firewood in (holds 4), light it with empty hands; each log burns `hoursPerLog` (4) on the host; its `Heat` ramps up over an hour and fades over three after it goes out. Firewood (`Kitchen/Firewood`, 3 per hand) only comes from Walt (15 in stock, 3 a day). The radio's second button gives the farm report (`Weather.Forecast()`, shown by `UI/Caption`): now, today and the next three days, warning of cold snaps and killing frost. Scene things that run on clock hours must reset their last-seen time on a clock jump (`IAfterLoad` and `DayClock.Jumped`), or a load spends days at once.
- Food (`Scripts/Food/`): `FreshFood` (produce, eaten raw) and `JarFood` (eat the whole jar, which leaves it empty) implement `IEdible`; holding one, the Pick up button says "Eat". `Perishable` ages food by clock hours (`shelfLifeDays` at room temperature) and darkens it past half-fresh; rotten food or an unsafe jar makes you sick instead of feeding you. A sealed jar doesn't spoil. `ColdStorage` is a trigger box (fridge rate about 0.3, freezer 0). Keep `Perishable` the last `IItemState` on a prefab so older saves line up.
- Multiplayer (survival branch, Netcode for GameObjects 2.x + Steamworks.NET): up to 4 players; whoever starts the game hosts it (solo play is hosting alone), and friends join through a friends-only Steam lobby (invite from the pause menu, or Join Game in Steam). `Scripts/Net/`: `NetSession` (on `Resources/NetSession.prefab` with the NetworkManager, UnityTransport and our `SteamTransport`), `HomesteadSession` (opening the Homestead hosts), `PlayerNet` (owner gets camera/input/HUD via `IPlayerBound`; others see a figure), `ItemNet` (on every item prefab: the host decides who holds what; the holder owns and moves it; state is shared via `IItemState` when it's let go), `ClockNet` (host keeps time; the night passes only when every player is in bed; the host saves when it ends), `Bed` (one sleeper each). Guests can only use things marked `ISharedUse` (items lying loose or in a basket, beds, the fridge/freezer doors, the garden, and the kitchen: stations, stove, pot, canner, sink, towels, shelves and labels). Items lying on a station are taken through the station. Kitchen sharing: whoever last handled an item runs it (`ItemNet.Simulates`; gate anything that changes over time with `ItemNet.Runs(this)`) and sends its states plus where it sits (parent path, container) every 0.3 s when they change; a non-owner may only send content changes (`ProposeRpc`), never where it sits. Applying a place reparents the item and calls `IAfterLoad.AfterLoad()` on the station there, just like loading. Scene ISaveables (cutting board, burners) use `SharedState` the same way, and a `SharedState` on a station also locks it to one player at a time. Remove items for good with `ItemNet.Remove`, never `Destroy`. The garden (`GardenNet` on each bed): the host grows it (nights, weeds, suckers, pests) and sends `GardenBed.Capture()` whenever it changes; guests apply only what changed (`ApplyShared`), and a guest's planting, watering, weeding and pinching show at once and go to the host through `GardenBed.Plant/Water/PullOut/TakeOff`. Ripe produce is spawned as shared items. A new item prefab needs NetworkObject (AutoObjectParentSync off, DontDestroyWithOwner on) + owner-authority NetworkTransform + ItemNet, and an entry in `Data/Network/NetworkPrefabs`. Copying a scene object with a NetworkObject duplicates its GlobalObjectIdHash: re-run NetworkObject.OnValidate and save. Sleepers lie in their bed (`Bed` turns off the CharacterController and places you; you get out on a free side) and collapsed players lie on the floor; other players see the figure lying down (`PlayerNet.Pose`). Test two players on one PC with a development build: host in the editor without Steam (create `Temp/GH_DIRECT`, delete it after), run the build with `-direct -join 127.0.0.1 -bot` (DevBot logs `[bot]` steps). Steam can't start twice in one process, so the editor keeps Steam running between Play sessions. The Steam app ID is 480 (Spacewar) in `steam_appid.txt` until the game has its own; copy that file next to built .exe files.
- Saving: a new item prefab needs a `SavedItem` key and an entry in `Data/SaveCatalog`; extra item state implements `IItemState`, scene systems implement `ISaveable`, and stations that hold things re-adopt them in `IAfterLoad`.
